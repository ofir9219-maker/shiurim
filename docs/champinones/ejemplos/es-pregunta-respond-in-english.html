Depth

The sugya ties dreams not only to what is seen, but to how it gets verbalized: the mouth (the interpreter’s words) concretizes the dream’s trajectory. Surface

Berakhot 11a uses זקנך (your beard), while Berakhot 55b uses זקן (an elder). This is the same Hebrew root ז-ק-נ, though the form differs (second-person possessive vs. noun).

Depth

In Berakhot 11a, Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya treats “beard” as an outward sign people read—something that can be praised and can also be weaponized (“against the destroyers”). In Berakhot 55b, the “zaken” is a source of tradition whose teaching defines what makes a dream effective: not the image alone, but the spoken reading of it. Taken together, “zaken” points to two kinds of legitimacy: external appearance (beard/age) and interpretive authority (an elder’s mouth/words). This is the closest literal champiñón we can verify from the provided texts, while the shiur indicates a stronger, missing link to Rabban Gamliel’s dream-text.

Synthesis

From the texts actually present here, the most concrete champiñón is the shared root ז-ק-נ (zaken): in Berakhot 11a it appears as “your beard has grown,” an outward sign that can influence others, and in Berakhot 55b it appears as “one elder,” a bearer of tradition whose teaching makes the decisive point that “all dreams follow the mouth.” That produces a limited but coherent yesod: external signs (like “beard/age”) generate authority in people’s eyes, but in the realm of dreams the determining power is often the verbal framing—the mouth that interprets. The shiur strongly asserts there is a much stronger connection specifically between Rabbi Elazar ben Azarya’s beard and Rabban Gamliel’s dream, but that key dream passage is not included in the provided Sefaria texts, so it cannot be built here as a fully verified champiñón.

Practical lesson

Do not confuse external markers of stature (e.g., looking like a “zaken”) with what truly gives something force; in many cases, the “mouth”—how we speak, interpret, and frame—determines how an experience lands and what it becomes.
