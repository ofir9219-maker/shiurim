Prayer as “et ratzon”: רצון across Berakhot and Tanakh

“Champiñones about prayer as 'et ratzón'”

The anchor is Berakhot 8a, where the verse “Va’ani tefillati… et ratzon” is read as a rule: the time of favor is when the congregation prays. The main champiñón is the literal phrase עת רצון shared by Psalms 69:14, Berakhot 8a:1, and Taanit 24b:6. A second hook is the shared root רצון with “yihyu le-ratzon” (Psalms 19:15) used as a liturgical seal of prayer in Berakhot 4b, reframing et ratzon as both timing and “acceptability.”

Layers

textual The repeated key-phrase עת רצון

The phrase עת רצון is the checkable mushroom: it appears in Tanakh (Psalms 69:14), is quoted and defined in Berakhot 8a, and is used in narrative form in Taanit 24b. The repetition is not conceptual first; it is literal in the wording.

structural Definition (Berakhot) + case study (Taanit)

Berakhot 8a is a derasha that defines et ratzon. Taanit 24b is a story where identifying “et ratzon” becomes the turning point that makes prayer effective, illustrating the definition in lived form.

conceptual Ratzon as timing and as acceptance

“Et ratzon” frames prayer as dependent on when, not only what. “Yihyu le-ratzon” frames prayer as dependent on being received/accepted, not only spoken. The shared root suggests these are one axis: favorable time is a mode of acceptability.

practical How to pursue “et ratzon”

The set pushes toward concrete alignment: pray with the tzibbur when possible (Berakhot 8a), and consciously seal your tefillah with a request for acceptance (Berakhot 4b’s verse). Taanit adds: be alert to openings that signal “now is the moment” for mercy.

Synthesis

“Et ratzon” is a word-first map for tefillah: the same phrase עת רצון moves from verse (Psalms) to definition (Berakhot 8a) to lived rescue (Taanit). The root רצון then echoes in the liturgical closing “yihyu le-ratzon,” suggesting that ‘favor’ is both a time-window and a state of being accepted. The yesod tying the set together is alignment: prayer becomes strongest when it aligns with a ציבור-time of ratzon, and it is framed—beginning and end—as a request that the words enter רצון before God.

Practical lesson

If you want your tefillah to be “et ratzon,” put it inside the congregation’s prayer-time when you can, and say “yihyu le-ratzon” as an intentional ask for acceptance—not as a throwaway closing line.
