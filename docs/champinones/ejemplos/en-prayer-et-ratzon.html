Et ratzon in prayer: tzibur, covenant, and “yehi ratzon”

“Champiñones about prayer as 'et ratzón'”

Berakhot 8a defines “et ratzon” (a favorable time) as the hour when the congregation prays, anchored in “Va’ani tefilati… et ratzon” (Psalms 69:14) and reinforced by “Be’et ratzon anitikha” (Isaiah 49:8). A story in Ta’anit 24b uses the same phrase “et ratzon” to trigger answered prayer for rain, and Berakhot 7a’s “Yehi ratzon” (God’s own ‘prayer’) supplies the inner engine of what makes a moment into ratzon: mercy overcoming anger. The shared word רצון (ratzon) and the exact Tanakh quotations create the champiñón; the depth is that communal prayer is not just ‘more people,’ but a time/space where Divine will is revealed and therefore response becomes possible.

Layers

textual A tight cluster around רצון / עת רצון / בעת רצון

Within a small set of sources, the word-root רצון repeats with minimal variation: “et ratzon” (Psalms 69:14; Berakhot 8a; Ta’anit 24b) and “be’et ratzon” (Isaiah 49:8; Berakhot 8a). The repetition is not accidental because Berakhot explicitly quotes the verses, and Ta’anit uses the same phrase as a practical conclusion.

structural Two directions: asking for ratzon vs testifying to ratzon

Psalms voices the human request (“let my prayer come… at a favorable moment”), while Isaiah voices the Divine response (“in a favorable time I answered you”). Berakhot places both in one sugya so that ‘et ratzon’ becomes a bridge between the human act of tefila and the Divine act of answering.

conceptual Et ratzon is communal time because it is answerable time

Berakhot’s definition “when the congregation is praying” is not merely sociological; it defines a time that carries a property: acceptability/answerability. Ta’anit’s rain narrative then functions like a case-study: identifying et ratzon turns “asking for mercy” into an answered request.

kabbalistic Et ratzon as Or flowing to Shekhinah (shiur layer)

In the shiur, et ratzon is described as the moment when Divine Or is mashpia toward the Shekhinah, and “asara shemitpalelim” is read as Shekhinah “imahem.” On that layer, communal prayer is et ratzon because it reveals the Shekhinah-bearing ‘place’ that receives the flow; the tzibur is not only quantity but a revelation of presence.

practical How to ‘aim’ for et ratzon

The Midrash makes the practical instruction explicit: pray with the tzibur so your tefila will be accepted. Ta’anit adds that when you sense an opening of ratzon, the right move is “ba‘u rachamei”—ask for mercy—matching the content of “yehi ratzon” in Berakhot 7a.

Synthesis

The champiñón here is the literal recurrence of רצון: “et ratzon” in Psalms (quoted in Berakhot 8a), “be’et ratzon anitikha” in Isaiah (quoted in Berakhot 8a), “et ratzon hi” in Ta’anit, and “yehi ratzon” in Berakhot 7a. On the surface, it looks like ‘timing’: find the favorable hour. But when the verses are allowed to interpret each other, “ratzon” becomes a shared yesod: a mode in which answer is possible—defined socially (the congregation’s tefila), witnessed covenantally (Isaiah’s promise), enacted historically (rain coming after “et ratzon”), and explained inwardly (God’s own “yehi ratzon” is mercy overtaking anger). Et ratzon in prayer is therefore not a trick for getting more outcomes; it is aligning one’s request with the revealed Divine will of rachamim, most stably accessed through the tzibur’s prayer.

Practical lesson

If you want your private prayer to touch “et ratzon,” attach it to the tzibur: pray with the congregation or at its time, and shape your requests in the direction of “yehi ratzon”—rachamim, softening din, and seeking good beyond strict deserving.
